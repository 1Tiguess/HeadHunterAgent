# 03 — Foundry

**Investor pitch deck plus the three-statement model behind it.**

## Problem

A founder raising a seed round has the story in their head and the numbers in a mess of
spreadsheet tabs. The deck and the model disagree, and an investor will notice within one
meeting. They need both artefacts produced together, from one set of assumptions.

## Who it's for

The founder presenting, and the analyst who will open the model afterwards and try to break
it.

## Scope

**Deck (.pptx), 12–15 slides:** problem, insight, product, why now, market sizing built
bottom-up, business model, traction with real numbers, competition positioned honestly,
go-to-market, team, financials, the ask and its use of funds. Speaker notes on every slide.
Consistent master, one type scale, charts native rather than pasted images.

**Model (.xlsx):** assumptions on their own clearly-marked sheet and nowhere else; monthly
P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow that actually tie; headcount plan driving payroll; unit
economics with CAC, LTV, payback; cohort retention; runway and the date cash hits zero;
a scenario switch across base/bull/bear.

## Out of scope

Cap table and dilution modelling, data room assembly, investor CRM, legal documents.

## Constraints

Deck must survive being opened in Google Slides and Keynote. Model must be formula-driven
throughout — no hardcoded values outside the assumptions sheet. Every chart in the deck
traces to a cell range in the model.

## Success criteria

- Balance sheet balances in every month of every scenario
- Cash flow ties to the P&L and to the balance sheet's cash line
- Flipping the scenario switch updates the deck's headline numbers with no manual editing
- No slide carries more than one idea
- Every number in the deck is findable in the model

## The hard part

Two distinct crafts, both with established technique: building a three-statement model
that ties, and building a deck that survives an investor's skepticism. But the technique is
mostly file-format mechanics plus financial modelling convention.

## Predicted verdict

**Phase 2 clear — Tier 0 hit.** `pptx` covers deck construction, masters, layouts, native
charts and speaker notes. `xlsx` covers formula-driven workbooks, multi-sheet models and
charting. Together they cover the deliverable. The genuinely hard residue — what belongs
in a seed deck, how a three-statement model ties — is domain knowledge I can state without
a hunt, which `triage-rubric.md:46` says to write down and build with rather than
ceremonially research.
